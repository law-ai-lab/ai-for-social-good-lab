# Ethics, Privacy, and Accessibility — v0.1

**Owner:** Rie Nakahara · **2026-10-16** · Applies to research and any later service design.

## Autonomy and essential assistance

- Treat adults as decision-makers. Ask what support they want; do not infer preferences from age, disability, living arrangement, or digital ability.
- Preserve choice among store visits, assistance, mobile retail, delivery, and combinations. Chosen interdependence can support independence.
- Never make essential help conditional on volunteering, sharing knowledge, earning support credits, taking part in research, or donating personal/research data.
- Participation, information sharing, provider contact, and any later research use require separate, specific, understandable consent. Refusal or withdrawal must not reduce access to essential assistance.

## Before any discovery conversations

Explain who is conducting the research, its exploratory purpose, duration, voluntary nature, what will be recorded, who can see it, and how to stop or request deletion. This is research, not a promise of transport or care. Use plain language, adequate time, and an accessible format. Confirm understanding without pressuring agreement.

Default to minimal written notes and no audio/video recording. Record only what is necessary; do not collect exact home addresses, medical histories, financial details, or account credentials. Use broad descriptions and participant codes privately. Avoid identifiable stories or quotes in GitHub, including combinations of details that could identify a person. Public Git history can retain previously committed content, so review before every commit.

Before collecting any participant data, define a private access-controlled location, named access holders, retention/deletion dates, withdrawal method, and consent record. None has been established in Mission #001, so **participant data collection has not begun**. De-identification reduces risk but is not a guarantee of anonymity. Do not put identifying raw notes into public issues or external AI systems.

Do not recruit someone unable to provide informed consent under this lightweight process. Pause and seek appropriate ethics/professional guidance and an accessible consent process rather than accepting convenience consent. A relative's preference must not automatically substitute for the adult's wishes.

## Safe research and provider contact

Do not ask participants to attempt unsafe journeys, carry heavy bags for a test, disclose credentials, or relinquish transport they rely on. Begin with retrospective accounts and public-document review. No transport or medical safety is certified by this Lab.

Obtain informed consent before contacting a support provider about a person or sharing their information; confirm exactly what will be shared and why. No provider or participant contact is authorized or performed as part of Mission #001. Public information may be researched without representing a person.

## Accessibility and human support

Account for people who cannot use smartphones, websites, apps, or voice interfaces. Future participation and support should offer suitable human-assisted, paper, telephone, or in-person routes according to individual needs; none is currently offered by this Lab. Consider vision, hearing, mobility, dexterity, literacy, language, and cognitive accessibility without stereotyping.

AI cannot replace safe transport, human care, safeguarding, or professional oversight. Verify a provider's current coverage, eligibility, costs, booking channels, accessibility, assistance limits, and return arrangements before claiming availability. Historical service descriptions are not recommendations to a particular person.

## Public integrity and future development

Clearly distinguish source-reported evidence, hypotheses, unknowns, and research decisions. Label all fictional examples. No fabricated participants, testimonials, statistics, or service outcomes. Publish corrections transparently and keep third-party source rights separate from original Lab writing.

Any later commercial exploration must preserve these safeguards, disclose conflicts, and obtain fresh consent for changed data uses. Public-interest purpose does not itself justify personal-data collection. **Advanced inside. Simple outside.** Complexity behind the scenes must never conceal consequential choices or remove accessible human explanations.
