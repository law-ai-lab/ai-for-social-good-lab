## Initial cognitive-health evidence scan
Checked 2026-10-16. This is not a systematic review or medical advice. No study below evaluates this Lab's product.

**E1 — WHO, Risk reduction of cognitive decline and dementia, second edition, published July 15, 2026.**
[Official publication](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240123557) · [Recommendations, sections on cognitive and social activity](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK623908/).

This evidence-based guideline synthesizes research and grades recommendations. It conditionally recommends social activity interventions for adults with normal cognition or mild cognitive impairment; overall certainty for that recommendation is very low. Cognitive stimulation is also conditionally recommended. This supports further investigation of everyday activities, not a claim about an AI shopping app. Intervention definitions, follow-up, populations, and outcomes vary; much evidence is observational. General social participation cannot be assumed equivalent to talking with a virtual character. Use the 2026 edition rather than presenting the 2019 social-activity position as current.

**E2 — Woods et al., Reminiscence therapy for dementia, Cochrane, March 1, 2018, DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD001120.pub3.**
[Original review and abstract](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD001120_reminiscence-therapy-dementia).

Systematic review of 22 randomized trials (1,972 people with dementia), searches through April 2017. Effects varied by setting and modality. Overall self-reported quality of life showed little important difference; cognition showed a very small immediate benefit of doubtful clinical importance, with little or no overall longer-term difference. Some communication outcomes improved; results and certainty differed. These are therapeutic reminiscence interventions in people already living with dementia, not prevention trials or evidence for spontaneous AI conversations with community-dwelling adults. Historical evidence is relevant context, not a complete current review.

**Design inference, not a research finding:** offer optional enjoyable choice and conversation without medical framing; investigate actual experience and confusion. Do not infer prevention from short-term cognitive scores, an engagement measure, or a pleasant demonstration.

## Validation and ethics
Before voluntary participant work, establish accessible consent, private minimal notes, deletion/withdrawal arrangements, and no effect on essential assistance. No data collection or recruitment is performed by this document. Avoid identifiable quotes and narratives in GitHub.

Suggested nonclinical observations: completion without unwanted assistance, clarity of next step, control over skip/exit, understanding of virtual/fictional labels, and optional self-reported enjoyment or discomfort. Keep these separate from health outcomes and do not equate completion with real-world safe transport.
