## 1. Scope correction and research question

This revision supersedes the Koshigaya-only Mission #002 published earlier on October 19. Koshigaya was an assistant-selected example, **not a founder-selected research location or project requirement**. The earlier version remains in Git history. Its valid findings are retained briefly as a supplementary local example below. Mission #001 remains unchanged.

**Research question:** How do existing approaches across Japanese regions support grocery access, personal choice and opportunities for enjoyable shopping, and what practical limitations remain when people discover, choose and use them?

Japan is the initial research environment. The founder's long-term vision is internationally adaptable, with older adults as the initial research and accessibility priority, without restricting the eventual product to one country or age group. This review does not establish overseas need, feasibility or service availability.

**Labels:** **E** = an original source describes a rule or arrangement; **H** = a hypothesis or possible benefit; **U** = unknown; **D** = a provisional research/design decision; **V** = founder-defined value. E verifies what a source publishes, not live capacity, individual suitability, safety, satisfaction or effectiveness.

## 2. Method and selection limits

We purposively selected six documented examples spanning Hokkaido, Tohoku, Kansai, Shikoku and Kyushu. They illustrate mobile retail, shopping transport, supermarket delivery, human assistance, cooperative delivery, and consolidated delivery. They are not a representative national sample, exhaustive market review, ranking or recommendation.

Sources are municipal publications, provider pages and delivery terms, with an older government case report used only as historical corroboration. All links were opened on October 19, 2026. Publication/update dates are recorded where visible; an undated page or crawl date must not be presented as a publication date. No resident address, account, order, booking, interview, service trial or provider contact was used. Local capacity and practical accessibility still require confirmation.

Service descriptions are usually strong evidence for published conditions, but weak evidence for lived experience or comparative benefit. No reviewed source establishes that our proposed app improves independence or enjoyment. No clinical or cognitive benefit is inferred.

**Findings that challenge assumptions:** Existing services already include non-digital ordering and, in at least one documented transport example, return travel and bag assistance. “No smartphone means no delivery” and “shopping support always ignores the return journey” are not defensible generalizations. Conversely, a published option does not prove usable local capacity. We must distinguish missing services, physical barriers, costs, information difficulty and a person's informed preference not to use an option.

## 5. Senior housing and consolidated grocery delivery

**E:** J2 explicitly includes older-adult facilities among mobile-retail visit settings. J11 documents consolidated group delivery and separately addresses certain facility-use registrations subject to conditions. These are different arrangements; neither demonstrates our proposed senior-housing shopping hub.

**H:** Residents might enjoy either private shopping or an optional shared lounge, with individually chosen products delivered together. **U:** We have not verified a named senior-housing operator offering our proposed workflow, a retailer partnership, individual order separation, room delivery, consent procedures, staffing or net cost savings.

Before any facility scenario is presented as real, verify resident choice, operator permission, retailer agreement, membership/payment arrangements, responsibility for chilled/frozen goods, sorting errors, storage, accessibility and the final handover. Meal provision, mobile retail, group grocery delivery and personal grocery ordering must not be treated as interchangeable. Residents must retain a private-shopping alternative; participation must not affect access to essential support.

## 7. Refined problem statement and international implications

**Working target:** An older adult who wants control over food choices and shopping method, but encounters a practical constraint in travel, carrying, ordering or receiving groceries. People who enjoy and can use existing arrangements are equally important comparison cases.

**Fictional scenario — not a participant account:** A person wants seasonal ingredients for a favorite meal, enjoys comparing products, and sometimes needs help carrying heavier purchases. On one day they may choose a store visit with selected help; on another, mobile retail, catalogue ordering or supermarket delivery. We do not assign a real address, disability, income, preference or service eligibility.

**Updated problem statement — H:** People may struggle to identify an option that fits both their desired shopping experience and their practical circumstances, even where some services exist. This review does not establish the prevalence or relative importance of that difficulty. Information cannot create unavailable transport, staffing, delivery coverage or affordable groceries.

**Provisional failure point — D:** Investigate the mismatch between a preferred shopping experience and the complete, usable fulfillment journey, including the return with bags or final delivery handover. Retain Mission #001's return-with-bags question, but do not presume it is the dominant barrier everywhere.

**International vision — V / D:** Reuse questions and an adaptable information structure, not Japanese eligibility rules. Future country-specific research must verify local retailers, transportation, geography, language, currency, digital/non-digital channels, payments, consumer rules, privacy obligations and support arrangements. No overseas service comparison has been conducted here; these are transfer questions, not legal conclusions or international rollout commitments. Older adults remain the first accessibility priority, while the long-term concept may benefit other ages.

## 8. Joyful virtual shopping and the smallest credible prototype

**V:** Shopping should offer enjoyment, discovery, social connection, memories and choice. These are founder-defined aims, not validated preferences or benefits of our product. The project is independent of ClearJP.

The canonical [Idea Parking Lot](idea-parking-lot.md) retains virtual supermarkets, smart glasses/accessible devices, remote shopping with family/friends, optional fictional AI characters and virtual pets, genuine authorized food memories and recipes, sensory associations, retailer-funded access, and consolidated senior-community delivery. No reviewed service establishes that those concepts are effective, wanted or financially feasible. An ordinary browser cannot physically reproduce smell or taste. Sponsorship and free delivery remain funding hypotheses; do not promise zero-cost real fulfillment.

**D — v0.1 recommendation, not new implementation:** Keep the December 31 target and a small Japanese browser demonstration. Proposed flow:

1. Welcome: 「今日はどんなお買い物を楽しみたいですか？」 with a direct 「買い物の方法を確認する」 route.
2. Optionally browse a few clearly fictional departments/products or a fictional recipe idea; allow skipping all entertainment and companionship.
3. Choose an intention: visit a shop, shop closer to home, or obtain groceries at home. Compare a small set of dated, source-linked arrangements with costs, channels and explicit unknowns.
4. Review the return/bag or delivery-handover checklist; print or save a plain summary and follow an official enquiry route. Do not display a booked ride, accepted order or promised delivery.

Japan-wide **research** does not require nationwide prototype coverage. A later demonstration dataset may use one manageable, justified area; none is selected by this document. International adaptability is a design consideration, not a multilingual launch commitment.

No accounts, payments, real ordering, live booking, persistent memories, sensitive information, medical scoring or real-person recreation are needed. Any optional character is clearly fictional/scripted and removable. Defer smart glasses, live video shopping, retailer integrations and housing operations. Assess whether a maintained static interface adds value before considering AI.

Safeguards: adult-to-adult language; readable text, keyboard navigation, voice-independent controls and print access; no essential assistance conditional on volunteering, knowledge sharing, credits or data donation; informed consent before sharing personal information or contacting providers; no identifiable participant data or private memories in GitHub. Chosen supporters must not take over decisions. Virtual company must not replace human relationships. Follow the [existing ethics principles](../ethics-privacy/principles.md).

## 9. Limits, decision gate and next 30–60-minute task

**What remains unknown:** User preferences and joy; actual accessibility; current service capacity; basket-level affordability; suitability for sensory, mobility or digital differences; use by people outside selected routes; real institutional savings; and whether existing pages already meet the information need. No service outcomes, participant counts, health effects or demand statistics were generated by the Lab.

Fees and timetables change. Some sources are undated or promotional; the dated Todock change shows why maintenance is necessary. A readable website does not provide offline support by itself. A print option or supporter route must be tested, not simply labeled inclusive. Provider narratives of enjoyable conversation are not controlled evidence of improved well-being.

**Next task — one fictional journey audit, not Mission #003:**

- **Purpose:** Test whether explaining existing options can address a concrete information gap while preserving shopping choice.
- **0–10 minutes:** Choose a justified candidate area and fictional shopping intention, without a residential address. Compare a store-visit route with an at/near-home option; do not select Koshigaya by default.
- **10–30 minutes:** Check one complete return/hand-over workflow from official/provider documents: access, eligibility, booking/ordering channel, timing, remaining costs, bags and final carrying. Mark missing facts unknown.
- **30–45 minutes:** Compare the original service pages with a paper comparison screen; include a successful-existing-service case and a case where information cannot solve the physical/service gap.
- **45–60 minutes:** Record discrepancies, source dates, optional-joy questions and a keep/revise/reject decision for the information concept. Do not treat the researcher's walkthrough as user validation.

**GitHub-ready deliverable:** a short, source-linked scenario audit proposed under `research/`, with no new app code. **Completion criteria:** both options traced, unknown steps visible, costs and source dates recorded, entertainment skippable, no fabricated outcome, and explicit questions for a later consent-based older-adult study. This task is recommended, not executed or scheduled here.
