## 1. Overall Vision

Develop a joyful, accessible virtual shopping experience that helps people continue enjoying shopping regardless of age, mobility, location, or digital ability.

Older adults are the initial priority, but the experience should eventually be enjoyable and useful for people of all ages.

Shopping is not merely obtaining necessities.

It is also about discovery, food, pleasure, friendship, memories, independence, and enjoying life.

### Core principles

- Advanced inside. Simple outside.
- Safety is the foundation. Joy is the purpose.
- Preserve independence, dignity, and personal choice.
- Make technology enjoyable rather than intimidating.
- Enable companionship without requiring it.
- Keep essential shopping access affordable and inclusive.
- Clearly distinguish real people from virtual AI characters.
- Protect privacy, consent, and personal memories.

## 💡 Idea #001 — Smart-Glasses Virtual Grocery Shopping

**Concept:** Allow customers to experience shopping in a virtual supermarket while sitting comfortably at home, potentially using smart glasses, VR, or other suitable devices.

Customers could browse shelves, examine products, choose groceries, and eventually order items for home delivery.

**Key hypothesis:** A familiar store-like experience may be easier and more enjoyable for some people than conventional online shopping.

Research usability, accessibility, device comfort, costs, motion sickness, and technical feasibility.

Smart glasses must be optional. Ordinary browser and screen-based access should remain available.

**Status:** Future hardware and immersive-experience research.

---

## 💡 Idea #002 — Accessible Devices as a Service

**Concept:** Customers should not need to purchase expensive equipment to participate.

Retailers, technology providers, housing communities, or other organizations could supply, lend, or maintain suitable shopping devices.

### Potential options

- Borrowed smart glasses.
- Shared tablets or headsets.
- Preconfigured devices.
- Repair and technical support.
- Personal devices already owned by customers.

Research maintenance, sanitation, device replacement, training, damage protection, and actual accessibility.

**Status:** Distribution and accessibility model requiring validation.

---

## 💡 Idea #003 — Inclusive Virtual Shopping Membership

**Concept:** Explore how organizations could finance the shopping experience and equipment through bundled services.

Possible arrangements include retailer memberships, institutional contracts, sponsorships, and community-supported access.

For example, senior housing providers could offer the experience as a resident amenity.

**Important clarification:** The founder does not want older adults to pay a separate platform membership fee.

Any membership-based arrangement should therefore be funded by participating businesses or institutions, not imposed as an additional charge on older shoppers.

**Status:** Exploratory institutional funding model.

## 💡 Idea #004 — Retailer-Funded Virtual Shopping Platform

**Concept:** Build a business-to-business-to-consumer (B2B2C) model in which retailers and participating organizations fund a consumer-facing virtual shopping platform.

### Potential funders

- Supermarkets.
- E-commerce businesses.
- Device manufacturers.
- Senior housing operators.
- Sponsors and promotional partners.
- Municipalities and nonprofit organizations.

Businesses could fund participation through service contracts, promotional campaigns, or other partnership arrangements.

Customers could enjoy virtual shopping without paying an additional platform fee.

**Safeguard:** Sponsored products must be distinguishable from independent recommendations. Free access must not require intrusive data collection, unwanted advertising, or unnecessary purchasing.

**Status:** Business-model hypothesis, not a validated revenue plan.

## 💡 Idea #005 — Pay for Groceries, Not Access

**Founder-defined affordability goal:**

Older adults should pay only for the groceries they choose to buy.

The intended experience should involve:

- No separate platform fee.
- No separate device rental fee.
- No separate maintenance fee.
- No additional technology-support charge.
- Ideally, no customer delivery fee.

Delivery costs still need to be funded somehow.

Research whether participating retailers, housing providers, sponsors, public programs, or community organizations could cover these expenses sustainably.

Avoid hidden charges or undisclosed increases in product prices.

This is a target service model, not an operating promise.

**Status:** Confirmed founder preference; financial feasibility unverified.

## 💡 Idea #006 — Community Shopping Hub

**Concept:** Enable senior housing communities and suitable residential facilities to consolidate grocery deliveries.

Residents could place individual orders, while a supermarket delivers multiple orders to one shared destination.

This might reduce delivery cost per resident.

Actual savings depend on order volume, staffing, temperature-controlled handling, individual sorting, final distribution, and contractual arrangements.

### Two shopping experiences

#### A. Private Shopping

Residents shop independently from their own rooms.

They may choose to shop alone or with an optional AI character, memories, or remotely connected loved ones.

#### B. Social Shopping Lounge

Residents enjoy shopping together in a shared community space.

They can discuss recipes, discover foods, socialize, and participate in a group activity.

Both experiences should preserve individual purchasing decisions, consent, and privacy.

Private and social modes should remain optional.

**Potential partners:** Independent senior housing, retirement communities, assisted-living facilities where appropriate, supermarkets, and community organizations.

**Status:** Future partnership and service-delivery research.

## 💡 Idea #007 — Remote Shared Shopping

**Concept:** Allow people living far apart to enjoy shopping together in real time, similar to a Zoom or online-meeting experience.

### Potential participants

- Parents and adult children.
- Grandparents and grandchildren.
- Friends and siblings.
- Neighbors.
- Residents of different communities.
- Support workers and older adults, with appropriate boundaries.

### Possible features

- Join the same virtual store.
- Explore products together.
- Talk through audio or video.
- View or share shopping areas.
- Discover foods and recipes.
- Discuss personal preferences.
- Enjoy seasonal shopping activities.
- Help one another without taking over decisions.

### Design requirements

- Participation is optional.
- Joining another person's session requires consent.
- Users control what information they share.
- Each person retains control of their own purchases.
- Shopping together must not become monitoring or surveillance.
- Private shopping remains available.
- Users can leave a session easily.

A clearly identified AI character could offer optional company when a real person is unavailable.

**Status:** Promising future social-shopping feature, not yet user-validated.

## 2. Related Product Vision — Memories and Virtual Friendship

The wider product vision also includes:

### Virtual AI Friendship

Optional friendly AI characters, including characters inspired by pets or significant people.

AI must never claim to be an actual person or a deceased loved one.

### Authentic Food Memories

Optional use of genuine, user-approved memories, family recipes, photographs, stories, and meaningful food traditions.

### Sensory Experiences

Explore how images, conversations, food descriptions, tastes, and smells can evoke memories and enjoyment.

Ordinary web apps cannot directly reproduce physical smell or taste.

### Joyful Cognitive Engagement

Investigate whether enjoyable planning, conversation, discovery, and reminiscence support engagement and quality of life.

Do not claim dementia prevention, cognitive improvement, or medical effectiveness without adequate research evidence.

These concepts require separate ethical and technical evaluation.

## 5. December 31, 2026 — Prototype v0.1

The approved near-term goal remains a publicly accessible, Japanese-language web prototype.

Keep it achievable.

### Recommended initial scope

Develop a lightweight, enjoyable virtual grocery-shopping experience that includes:

1. A welcoming, store-like interface.
2. Simple navigation among a few fictional shopping departments.
3. Easy selection of example products.
4. An optional clearly fictional AI-style character interaction or scripted demonstration.
5. A demonstration of private shopping.
6. A lightweight concept of shopping together, if time permits.
7. A practical grocery-access support scenario based on verified research.
8. Clear disclosure that this is a prototype and does not process real purchases or guarantee deliveries.

Use synthetic products, prices, and scenarios unless real data and permissions are available.

### Defer until later

- Actual smart-glasses integration.
- Live retailer ordering and payment processing.
- Real grocery delivery.
- Device distribution and rental.
- Real multi-user video shopping infrastructure.
- Persistent sensitive personal memories.
- AI recreations inspired by identifiable deceased people.
- Institutional commercial contracts.

The first prototype should demonstrate the experience, not pretend to operate an unbuilt delivery network.

### Recommended minimum demonstration

Proposed path: **welcome → choose private shopping → explore 2–3 fictional departments → select a few example products → view an example shopping list → choose how to obtain groceries → review one researched local-access scenario and confirmation checklist**.

Department and product counts are suggested scope caps, not approved requirements. Use standard screen navigation; no 3D movement, account, precise home address, medical input, live inventory, checkout, or real order. Synthetic prices must be labeled examples wherever displayed, including totals. Use “example shopping list,” not “order confirmed.” Clearly separate fictional product content from real, dated service information.

The practical-access route must remain directly reachable without browsing products, choosing a character, sharing memories, or entering a group. Optional character dialogue should be labeled fictional/scripted and easy to dismiss. A social-shopping concept must not imply an active remote participant or actual connection. No real-person likeness, private recordings, or sensitive memories are needed.

This is a scoped recommendation for review, not authorization to expand implementation. It brings the founder's later store-like proposal together with the earlier practical-access flow without deleting its direct-help route.
